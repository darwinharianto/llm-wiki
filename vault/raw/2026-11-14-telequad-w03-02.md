In turn, each subframe consists of 1/2/4/8/16 slots -shown in different colors- depending on the selected SCS/numerology. As for the time domain, it is divided in 10ms radio frames, each on consisting in 10 subframes of 1ms each, as shown in the picture below. A Resource Element (RE) is defined as a unit of one subcarrier (frequency domain) and one OFDM symbol (time domain). An NR channel bandwidth consists of a number of RBs. A consecutive series of 12 subcarriers forms one Resource Block (RB). This is referred to as "multiple numerologies". The SCS is the distance between the centres of two consecutive subcarriers, and the possible values for SCS are (in kHz): 15; 30; 60; 120 and 240. To allow for such a flexibility, NR uses a flexible frame structure, with different Subcarrier Spacings (SCS). To cope with various deployment scenarios, NR supports a wide range of carrier frequencies (within two possible ranges) and channel bandwidths, as shown in the table below. As a complement waveform with lower peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR) to improve UL coverage, DFT-s-OFDM (OFDM with Discrete Fourier Transform precoding) can be used in the uplink although limited to single-layer transmission only. In contrast to LTE, OFDM can also be used in the NR uplink (UL) direction.
